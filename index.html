Two days in Istanbul, built around the food worth eating and the sights worth the time.

You land at dawn on Saturday and fly out Monday evening, which is close to three full days. Base at The Bank in Karaköy, where both dinners are a walk from the door, cross the bridge for the Old City, and take the metro to the airport at 16:15 Monday. No tourist restaurants, no museum pass, nothing that needs a tour bus.

Saturday, land at dawn and take the Old City

January 2Karaköy, Cihangir, Sultanahmet, Fatih, Beyoğlu

The long day. You'll be running on airplane sleep, so the plan front-loads the big sights while you're still adrenalized, builds in a nap at six, and ends with the one dinner that can't move.

  1. 06:30Land Istanbul. Passport control, no visa on a US passport, collect your bag. Buy an Istanbulkart at the kiosk by the M11 metro entrance, card about $3.50, load 300 lira. Early Saturday is the one time the roads are empty, so a taxi makes sense today.
  2. 07:30Taxi to The Bank Hotel, Bankalar Caddesi, Karaköy, from the official rank at arrivals, orange cars. 45 minutes at this hour. Pay by card. about $45 to 50Metro alternative: M11 to Gayrettepe, M2 to Şişhane, 10 minutes downhill on foot. About 70 minutes, $2.
  3. 08:20Check in, or drop bags. Your card's early check-in is "if available", so email the hotel the week before with the 06:30 landing. In January they'll usually have the room. Shower if it's ready, otherwise freshen up and go.
  4. 09:15Van Kahvaltı Evi, Cihangir, a 15-minute walk uphill. The Kurdish breakfast spread: otlu peynir herb cheese, kavut, honey and kaymak, menemen, endless tea. Saturday queues form after 09:30, so you're just ahead of it. about $15The hotel breakfast is free with your card, but this is the one that's worth the walk. Save the hotel's for a coffee.
  5. 10:45Tram to Sultanahmet. T1 from Tophane, 15 minutes.
  6. 11:00Blue Mosque. Free. It closes around each prayer and the midday prayer is 13:13, so go now. Shoes off, knees and shoulders covered, scarves provided.
  7. 11:45Hagia Sophia. The visitor route is the upper gallery, entered on the northeast side opposite the Ahmed III fountain. In January the queue is minutes. Bring a layer, the gallery is unheated stone. about $28Basilica Cistern is next door at about $39 if you have energy, but it's the first thing to drop. Monday has a slot for it too.
  8. 13:30Siirt Şeref Büryan, Kadınlar Pazarı, Fatih. Tram to Aksaray and a 10-minute walk, or 25 minutes on foot. Büryan, lamb pit-roasted in a sealed oven, and perde pilavı, rice baked inside a pastry veil. The square is lined with Siirt and Bitlis places, this is the original. about $15
  9. 14:45Vefa Bozacısı, a 10-minute walk, open since 1876. Boza is fermented millet, thick and tart, with roasted chickpeas on top. January is its season. Cash. about $3
  10. 15:00Süleymaniye Mosque, five minutes up the hill. Sinan's masterpiece, free, calmer than the Blue Mosque, and the terrace has the best view of the Golden Horn. Afternoon prayer is 15:34, so be in by 15:05 and out by 15:25, or see the terrace first and the interior after 16:15.
  11. 15:45Grand Bazaar, a 15-minute walk down through the book market at Beyazıt. Open until 19:00 today, closed tomorrow, so this is the visit. Go in at Beyazıt, drift toward the old bedesten in the middle, and ignore anyone who opens with where you're from. Snack if you want one: a dürüm at Aynen Kebap inside the bazaar, where the shopkeepers eat, about $7. Dönerci Şahin Usta is just outside the Nuruosmaniye gate and usually sells out by 17:00, so if you're there by 16:30 and hungry, that's the döner. Otherwise it's Monday's lunch. If you run out of time or energy, the bazaar is also open Monday morning, and the page has a fallback for that.
  12. 17:00Spice Bazaar, 15 minutes downhill, then Rüstem Paşa Mosque next door for the İznik tiles. Coffee beans from Kurukahveci Mehmet Efendi on the corner. Open every day, so if the Grand Bazaar ran long, this can slide to Sunday or Monday.
  13. 17:20Hatay Asi Künefeleri, Hasırcılar Caddesi 27, the lane running west from the Spice Bazaar, two minutes away. Künefe the Hatay way: shredded pastry, unsalted cheese brought from Hatay, butter, syrup, pistachio, served when the cheese still pulls. about $7Lezzet-i Şark Antep Sofrası at number 38 on the same lane does a lighter-syrup version if Asi is full.
  14. 17:50Walk home over the Galata Bridge as the light goes, sunset is 17:47, 12 minutes to the hotel. Nap. Ninety minutes, alarm set. This is what makes dinner work.
  15. 19:45Asmalı Cavit, Asmalımescit, Beyoğlu. Tünel funicular up from Karaköy, five minutes from the hotel, then a short walk. The classic meyhane: cold mezes, then hot ones, then grilled fish or lamb, with rakı. Closed Sundays, packed on weekdays, so reserve for tonight. about $35 to 45 with rakıBackup: Zübeyir Ocakbaşı, a 10-minute walk, still a top grill but pricier and more touristy than it was.
  16. 21:45Mandabatmaz, Olivya Geçidi off İstiklal. The city's consensus Turkish coffee, thick enough that the proverbial buffalo won't sink. Then Tünel down. about $3Still want a second künefe? Keyfeder Künefe Katmer, Çukurlu Çeşme Sokak off İstiklal, is open until 02:00.
  17. 22:30The Bank's rooftop bar, one drink looking at the Golden Horn, on the $100 property credit. Bed.

Sunday, the palace, then across the water

January 3Karaköy, Topkapı, Kadıköy, the Bosphorus

Topkapı first thing, before the tour groups. Then Kadıköy, where Istanbul eats on its own time, and the public ferry's own Bosphorus boat in the afternoon light. Grand Bazaar and the meyhane are closed today, which is why they were yesterday.

  1. 07:45Karaköy Güllüoğlu, two minutes from the hotel. What locals come for at breakfast: su böreği, the layered water pastry with cheese, and tea. Opens around 07:00. about $8Fallback if you oversleep: the hotel breakfast, free with your card.
  2. 08:30Tram to Gülhane. T1 from Karaköy, three stops, 10 minutes, then five minutes through the park to the palace gate.
  3. 09:00Topkapı Palace with the Harem and Hagia Irene. Be at the gate at opening. Two hours. The Harem is the part worth the add-on. about $55Skip the Museum Pass. At $115 it doesn't pay back for this itinerary.
  4. 11:15Ferry Eminönü to Kadıköy. Ten minutes' walk to the pier, 20 minutes across, Istanbulkart. Tea on board from the cart. about $1.30
  5. 11:50Kadıköy market, Güneşlibahçe Sokak: the fish stalls, the pickle shops, the olive men. Turkish coffee at Fazıl Bey, then stuffed mussels at Midyeci Yasin on Moda Caddesi, eaten standing. about $7 for both
  6. 12:30Çiya Sofrası, Güneşlibahçe Sokak 43. Point at the pots: Gaziantep yuvalama, stuffed artichokes, lamb with quince, whatever the kitchen has from the southeast that day. No alcohol. The one restaurant that cooks the whole of Anatolia under one roof. about $18Alternative: Yanyalı Fehmi, since 1919, new Bib Gourmand in the 2026 guide, for esnaf stews and rice. About $20.
  7. 13:30Baylan, Muvakkithane Caddesi. Kup griye, the caramel, ice cream and almond sundae this 1923 patisserie invented. about $10
  8. 14:00Ferry Kadıköy to Eminönü, 20 minutes, to make the boat below.
  9. 14:40Şehir Hatları Short Bosphorus Tour from Eminönü. Two hours up past Dolmabahçe, Ortaköy, the bridges and the Rumeli fortress, back at 16:35 in the last of the light. The public ferry line's own tour, not a tout's boat. The only winter sailing, so don't miss the 14:00 ferry. about $7
  10. 16:45Walk the Galata Bridge lower deck past the fishermen as the sun sets at 17:47, then through the Karaköy fish market to the hotel. Galata Tower is a 7-minute climb from the hotel if you want the view, about $34, last daytime ticket 18:14. Optional.
  11. 19:30Ali Ocakbaşı Karaköy, Kardeşim Sokak 45, a hidden lane off Tersane Caddesi, five minutes from the hotel. Bib Gourmand two years running. Sit at the counter in front of the charcoal. This is your Adana: hand-minced lamb on the wide skewer, served on lavaş with grilled onions and pomegranate, then lamb şiş and chicken wings if you have room. Reserve. about $35 to 45Beware the copycat on the waterfront called Chef Ocakbaşı. Quieter alternative: Karaköy Lokantası, the turquoise-tiled Bib Gourmand, open Sundays from 16:00, about $30.
  12. 21:30Walk home, five minutes. Rooftop if there's credit left.

Monday, Balat, the döner, and out

January 4Beyoğlu, Balat and Fener, Karaköy, then the airport

The 20:35 flight gives you the whole day. Ask for late checkout with your card and leave the bags until 16:00. Monday is the day for the city's best döner, which is shut Sundays, and the one neighborhood the other days didn't reach.

  1. 08:00Lades Menemen, Sadri Alışık Sokak just off İstiklal, Tünel up and a five-minute walk. Menemen, eggs scrambled slowly with tomato and pepper, with bread for mopping. Closed Sundays, open today from 06:00. about $7
  2. 09:15To Balat. Taxi from Tünel, 15 minutes, or Tünel down and the T5 tram from Eminönü to Fener, 30 minutes. about $8 by taxi
  3. 09:30Fener and Balat on foot. The Greek Orthodox Patriarchate at St George, the red-brick Phanar Greek College on the hill, the painted houses on Kiremit Caddesi, the Bulgarian Iron Church on the water. Ninety minutes, mostly uphill and down.
  4. 11:00Chora, the Kariye Mosque, 15 minutes' walk or a 5-minute taxi up to Edirnekapı. The finest Byzantine mosaics and frescoes in the city, reopened in 2024. Open Mondays, closed Fridays. about $22Swap: if you'd rather, the Basilica Cistern in Sultanahmet, about $39, or a second pass at the Grand Bazaar, open today from 08:30, 15 minutes by tram from the hotel.
  5. 12:15Taxi to Nuruosmaniye, 15 minutes back toward the Grand Bazaar. about $8
  6. 12:30Dönerci Şahin Usta, Kılıççılar Sokak 7, Nuruosmaniye, a few steps from the Grand Bazaar's Nuruosmaniye gate. Lamb döner stacked by hand and cut thin, in bread or on a plate with rice, with a glass of ayran. Standing room and a short queue at this hour. Closed Sundays, and gone when the spit is gone, usually by 17:00. about $8Backups: Nato Lokantası in Karaköy for the weekday tradesman's stews, by 13:00, about $10. Or Karadeniz Döner in Beşiktaş, the other famous spit, a 10-minute taxi from the hotel, lunch only.
  7. 13:10Grand Bazaar, second pass if Saturday ran short, you're at the gate. Otherwise T1 tram from Çemberlitaş to Karaköy, 15 minutes.
  8. 13:45Karaköy Güllüoğlu. Box a kilo of baklava for Karachi, and a last slice with tea. Order fıstıklı, the pistachio, and ask for it with kaymak. about $6, plus $45 for the box
  9. 14:30Mandabatmaz one more time, Tünel up, or the hotel rooftop for a coffee on whatever credit is left. Istanbul Modern is three minutes from the hotel if you want an hour indoors.
  10. 15:45Collect the bags. Baklava flat in the carry-on.
  11. 16:15Metro to the airport. Ten minutes uphill to Şişhane, or Tünel up and a three-minute walk. M2 to Gayrettepe, M11 to the airport. About 70 minutes, immune to the evening rush. about $2Taxi from the hotel at 16:15 is 60 to 90 minutes in Monday traffic, about $50. Only better if the metro feels like too much with bags.
  12. 17:30Istanbul Airport. Check-in closes 60 minutes before departure. Passport exit stamp, security. The iGA Lounge sells 3-hour passes for about $60 if you want a shower before the flight.
  13. 20:35Fly to Karachi, about 5 h 15 m, plus 2 hours of time change. Lands around 04:00 Tuesday.

If the Karachi flight leaves from Sabiha Gökçen on the Asian side rather than Istanbul Airport, the run is different: ferry to Kadıköy and the M4 metro to the airport, about 75 minutes, or a taxi of 60 to 90 minutes. Check the airport code on the ticket.

Before you go

The Pakistan visa is the only item with a real deadline. Everything else is a week-of job.

Safety, plainly: the US advisory for Turkey is Level 2, the same as much of Europe. Istanbul's risks are the ordinary ones. Pickpockets on packed trams and in the Grand Bazaar, the shoe-shine brush drop on Galata Bridge, the friendly stranger near Taksim who invites you for a drink, and taxis taking the long way. Pay taxis by card, stay out of demonstrations, and don't post anything political while you're there. Emergency number is 112.

The eat list

Every place on the itinerary, with the hour and the closed day that matter. Hours come from listings that disagree with each other, so call the two reservations and treat the rest as close enough.

Your list, placed. Döner is Monday lunch at Şahin Usta, with Saturday as a bonus if you reach the bazaar early. Künefe is Saturday at Hatay Asi, beside the Spice Bazaar. Baklava is Güllüoğlu, Sunday breakfast and a Monday box. Adana is Sunday night at the Ali Ocakbaşı counter.

Balon dürüm isn't in Istanbul. It belongs to the southeast, and nothing in the city that checked out serves it. The Değirmencioğlu names that come up here are unrelated: an ocakbaşı in Fenerbahçe that looks closed, a dürümcü in Güngören with poor reviews, and a tradesman's canteen in Sultanahmet. None is worth an hour in transit. Save it for a trip that goes to the source.
