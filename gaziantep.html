Jan 1 to 5, 2027, landing in Karachi before dawn on Jan 6 Solo. Four flights. One city to eat in.

Orlando to Karachi, with three days of eating in Gaziantep in between.

Istanbul is just the airport on this trip. Gaziantep is Turkey's UNESCO City of Gastronomy, two hours by air from Istanbul, and it is where beyran, küşleme, katmer and the country's best baklava actually come from. You eat there Saturday night through Tuesday lunch, then fly out to Karachi the same evening.

Day by day

Copper dots are meals. Black dots are flights. Prices are in US dollars at roughly 50 lira to the dollar, which is where the lira is expected to sit in January.

Day 1, leave Orlando

Friday, January 1

New Year's Day. Nothing to do but pack and get to the airport. Shift your sleep an hour or two earlier for the two nights before this so the eastward jump hurts less.

  1. MorningPack light. One carry-on plus a small bag leaves room for a kilo of baklava on the way out. Bring a real winter coat, Gaziantep is near freezing at night.
  2. ~14:30Orlando to New York JFK, about 2 h 45 m. Booked as one Turkish Airlines ticket all the way to Gaziantep, the JFK leg rides on a JetBlue or Delta codeshare and your bag is checked through.
  3. 20:40Turkish Airlines TK2, JFK to Istanbul, about 10 hours, lands 13:45 Saturday. Sleep as soon as you board. Dinner service comes fast, then the cabin goes dark.

Alternative routing if JFK prices badly: Orlando to Washington Dulles on United, then TK8 at 22:10, landing Istanbul 15:20. That still makes the 18:15 Gaziantep flight.

Day 2, land and keep going

Saturday, January 2Istanbul airport, then Gaziantep

Don't leave the airport in Istanbul. The city is 45 to 90 minutes each way and you'd get two hours there at most. Save it for a real visit and go eat where the food is from.

  1. 13:45Land Istanbul. Passport control, no visa needed on a US passport. Bags stay checked through on a single ticket. Walk to the domestic zone and clear domestic security, 20 minutes on foot.
  2. 14:45Cuisine Anatolia, airside near the A and B piers, if you need something. Keep it to soup. Real food is three hours away. about $10
  3. 16:05Turkish Airlines to Gaziantep, 1 h 45 m, lands 17:45. If the booking engine refuses the 2 h 20 m connection, take the 18:15 departure landing 19:55. Either works.
  4. 18:15Taxi to the old town, 25 minutes. Check in at Anadolu Evleri or Ali Bey Konağı, both restored Ottoman mansions inside the bazaar quarter. taxi about $15
  5. 20:00İmam Çağdaş, in Uzun Çarşı since 1887. Order Ali Nazik, a soğan kebabı, one lahmacun, and finish with their kuru baklava. Big, fast, and one of the few kebab houses that serves dinner. Jet lag will hit hard, eat and go to bed. about $17

Day 3, the slow Sunday

Sunday, January 3Gaziantep

Sunday is the one day the küşleme master and most bazaar shops close, so this day is museums, coffee, and the places that run seven days.

  1. 07:30Katmerci Zekeriya Usta, Bay Hilmi Sokak 16. Classic kaymaklı katmer, pistachio and clotted cream folded into paper-thin dough, with tea. The dough runs out before noon. Backup is Orkide Pastanesi for a full Antep breakfast spread. about $9
  2. 10:00Zeugma Mosaic Museum, the largest mosaic museum in the world and home of the Gypsy Girl. Two hours. Open daily in winter 08:30 to 17:00. about $13
  3. 12:30Çulcuoğlu Et Lokantası, Suyabatmaz branch by the Almacı spice market. Starters, patlıcan kebabı (eggplant and minced lamb), sebzeli kebap. Wide menu, open Sundays. about $15
  4. 15:00Tahmis Kahvesi, Şehitler Caddesi 25, pouring coffee since 1635. Order menengiç, the terebinth-berry coffee that only exists here, then an Osmanlı kahvesi. about $4
  5. 16:00Gaziantep Castle and Hışvahan at its foot. The castle's quake restoration was declared finished in December 2025 but reopening has slipped, so check locally. The han and the castle-foot lanes are open regardless.
  6. 17:00Koçak Baklava, Mücahitler main branch, open to 23:00. Three pieces: fıstıklı, midye, and şöbiyet, with tea. about $5
  7. 19:30Bayazhan, a restored 1909 han in Akyol with live music and a wine and rakı list. Mezze, then vişneli damat kebabı (sour-cherry kebab) or lamb over hummus. The splurge dinner. about $27

Day 4, the big eating day

Monday, January 4Gaziantep

Everything is open. Breakfast is the city's defining dish, lunch is the best kebab in Turkey, and you are carrying baklava home.

  1. 08:00Metanet Lokantası, Kozluca Caddesi 11. Beyran: lamb neck simmered twelve hours, rice, garlic and chili broth poured over it at the table, eaten with tırnak pide. Ask for acılı. They open at 05:00 and the pot runs low by 10:00. about $8
  2. 09:30Elmacı Pazarı and Bakırcılar Çarşısı, the 250-year-old spice lane and the coppersmiths' bazaar across from it. Buy pepper paste, Antep pistachios, dried eggplant for dolma. Then the Emine Göğüş Culinary Museum around the corner.
  3. 11:30Kebapçı Halil Usta, Karşıyaka, a 10-minute taxi behind the Zeugma museum. Küşleme, the lamb tenderloin that Gaziantep kebab is judged by, plus simit kebabı, kaşık salatası and ayran. Lunch only, shutters when the meat runs out, be seated by 12:30. about $12
  4. 15:00Elmacıpazarı Güllüoğlu, the original Güllüoğlu inside the spice market. Eat şöbiyet and sütlü nuriye here, then have a kilo boxed for travel. Koçak and İmam Çağdaş pack for flights too. about $5 to eat, $42 for the kilo
  5. 19:30Yesemek Gaziantep Mutfağı, Şekeroğlu. The home-cooking side of the city you can't get anywhere else: yuvalama (tiny chickpea-lamb dumplings in yogurt), içli köfte, ekşili ufak köfte. Open until 21:00. about $9

If you'd rather trade this day for a road trip: Şanlıurfa is 2 hours east by bus. Lamb-liver dürüm for breakfast at Ciğerci Aziz Usta, Göbeklitepe, then a sıra gecesi night of music and çiğ köfte for about $40. Return on the 07:00 bus Tuesday for a last Gaziantep lunch. Adana and Antakya each burn most of a day in transit and Antakya's food scene is still mostly in prefab stalls after the 2023 quake.

Day 5, out through Istanbul

Tuesday, January 5Gaziantep to Istanbul to Karachi

Book Gaziantep to Karachi as one Turkish Airlines ticket. Your bag checks through to Karachi and the booking engine will only sell you a connection that works.

  1. 08:00Sakıp Usta or Kelebek Paça Beyran, Alleben. Beyran round two, to compare with Metanet. Or Orkide Pastanesi for breakfast and a dondurmalı katmer, katmer with ice cream. about $8
  2. 10:00Last bazaar run for anything you forgot, then pack. Baklava goes in the carry-on, flat.
  3. 12:00Üçler Kebap, Sarıgüllük. A soğanlı and a sarımsaklı lahmacun, light enough to still want airplane dinner. about $4
  4. 13:00Check out, taxi to the airport, 25 minutes. about $15
  5. ~15:00Turkish Airlines, Gaziantep to Istanbul, 1 h 50 m, landing around 17:00. Exact departure depends on what Turkish sells against TK708 that day; expect a mid-afternoon flight and a 3 to 4 hour layover.
  6. 17:00Istanbul transfer. Walk from domestic arrivals to the international zone, exit passport control, security re-screen. Allow 90 minutes minimum, you'll have more. The iGA Lounge sells 3-hour passes for about $60 if you want a shower and a quiet seat. Turkish's own lounges don't sell day passes.
  7. 21:05Turkish Airlines TK708, Istanbul to Karachi, Airbus A330, 5 h 15 m. Lands Jinnah International at 04:20 Wednesday, January 6.

TK708 runs most days but not every day, and the sources disagree on which day it skips in the winter schedule. Confirm it operates Tuesday, January 5 before you book the hotel. If it doesn't, Pegasus PC130 leaves Sabiha Gökçen around 20:00 nightly, but that's a separate ticket from a different airport.

Before you go

The Pakistan visa is the only item with a real deadline. Everything else is a week-of job.

Safety, plainly: Gaziantep city is routinely visited and treated as safe. The US advisory is Level 2 for Turkey with a do-not-travel zone along the Syrian border 60 km south. Stay in the city, skip the Kilis direction, and it's quieter than Istanbul. Solo dining is the norm here, most lokantas have counter seats.

The eat list

Every place on the itinerary, with the hour that matters. Gaziantep's best kebab is a lunch thing and its best breakfasts are over by mid-morning. Plan the day around that and nothing else.

Opens 05:00, go before 10:00. About $8.
